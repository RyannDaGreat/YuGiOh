# Autopilot log — structure-deck Haiku tournament (2026-08-17)

Modes in force: **autopilot** (never block on the user; decide, log, keep moving)
and **bulldog** (never let go of the job until the whole 121-cell matrix is real).

Decisions taken without asking, and why:

1. **Field = the 11 decks with `category: "structure"`.** The user said "only
   structure decks! not curated", which is exactly the manifest's three-way
   category split, so the filter is `category === "structure"` rather than a
   hand-typed list: SDY, SDK, SDP, SD1, SD2, SD3, SD4, SD6, SD10, SDSC, SDMP.
   All eleven are `format: "classic"`, so every pairing is legal as-is.

2. **Grid semantics: row deck goes FIRST (P0), column deck second (P1).** The
   user asked that "each triangular half decides who goes first". Making the row
   the first player achieves that automatically: the pair {A,B} appears at (A,B)
   with A on the play and at (B,A) with B on the play, so the two halves are the
   two seatings. Documented in schedule.mjs and in the report header.

3. **All three games of every best-of-3 are played** (no early stop at 2–0). It
   removes coordination between the two agents of a cell and gives every cell the
   same amount of evidence. 121 cells x 3 = 363 duels = 726 Haiku agents.

4. **A driver script owns the agent pool, not this conversation.** 726 agents
   spawned from the session would spend the whole context on bookkeeping. The
   tournament's entire state lives in the duel records, so the driver is
   stoppable and resumable, and the session just babysits it.

5. **Random-play "rescue" removed after the user's objection.** No duel is ever
   resolved by random play or by comparing life points. `autoplay.mjs` is now
   calibration/fuzzing only and refuses to touch any `sdc-*` id.

6. **Forcing mechanism for abandoned duels** (user: "if this ever happens, come up
   with a mechanism to force it"): after any agent pair that fails to finish its
   duel, the driver runs `nudge` — a fresh single-decision Haiku agent on the seat
   the engine is waiting on, whose only job is to answer that one menu. Every menu
   has at least one legal answer, so the board always moves, and the decision is
   still a Haiku decision. Then a fresh pair resumes. A board that survives six
   focused agents is logged as `stuck` for inspection instead of being faked.

7. **The honor boundary is enforced, not requested.** A `PreToolUse` hook
   (`tools/seat-guard.sh`) restricts each seat's shell to `node bin/ygo.js …` for
   its own seat: no `--as all`, no other seat, nothing under `duels/` (the record
   contains the seed, hence the opponent's hand), no `undo`/`fork`, no
   `play … random`, no command chaining. Verified live: a seat's `cat duels/…` is
   blocked. The two duels played before the hook existed were deleted and replayed
   so all 363 run under identical conditions.

8. **No strategy briefs.** Both seats get the identical baseline from PLAYER.md,
   so the only asymmetry in the tournament is the decklist.

Measured before scaling: a complete duel runs 250–450 decisions and takes
8–9 minutes with two Haiku agents; 363 duels at 10 matches in flight projects to
roughly 5–6 hours.

9. **Scaled 10 -> 100 matches on the user's word** ("hyper-frenzy for haiku duelists"), after a clean
   stop: the driver was killed *and* its 19 orphaned agents killed before relaunching, because an
   orphan plus a new agent on the same seat could each answer the same menu and record two moves
   where the game called for one. The 10 part-played duels were simply resumed by the new run.
   Three changes made 100 matches work: async `seatPrompt` (a synchronous `ygo brief` spawn blocks
   the driver thread, and 100 matches call it 200 times at once), a 90-minute agent timeout (at this
   concurrency a healthy agent waits a long time for its opponent), and `wait --timeout 2700` in the
   seat prompt. Measured: load ~33 of 128 logical CPUs, ~70 GB of 480 GB, disk idle.

10. **Cost is capped per agent, not just per model** (user: "we don't want high cost"). `MODEL` is
    the one place a model is named and it is `haiku` for seat agents *and* nudge agents; every agent
    additionally gets `--max-budget-usd`. Verified at scale: every `--model` flag running on the
    machine was `haiku`. An agent that hits its cap stops and is replaced, so the cap never turns
    into an abandoned duel.

11. **Checked that the speed-up was real rather than degenerate.** At 99 finished duels: every single
    one ended "LP reached 0" (no deck-outs, no draws), with move counts spread 55–417 and a median of
    153 — a genuine distribution of real games, not agents bailing out early.
